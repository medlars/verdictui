### The skip count, and why it is zero

`grep -ci skipped` over the test log returns **12**, which reads as a silently
degraded suite — the `no.md` #58 defect, where a skipping channel and a passing
one are indistinguishable in the summary line.

All 12 are **test NAMES** containing the word
(`testNodesWithoutMetricsOrVisibilityAreSkipped`,
`testANodeWithoutTextMetricsIsSkippedRatherThanGuessedAt`, …) and every one
**passed**. Real skips: **zero**. This is why the classifier must anchor on whole
phrases rather than a bare `skipped` substring — a substring rule calls a fully
executed suite a skip, which is the same false reading in the opposite direction.

`LieCatchTests` and `CrossValidationTests` both PASSED, so the SD1 honesty gate
and the SD6 permission path genuinely executed rather than skipping past a
degraded window server.

One `RBSRequestErrorDomain Code=5 "Launch failed"` appears in the log for a
witness host; it was retried and the suite is green. Recorded rather than
dismissed — it is the same LaunchServices pressure CTS-B7BB8A8D tracks.

## MCP fuzz surface — verified, not assumed

`TestMCPInputSurfaceSurvivesHostileInput` exists and drives the **release binary**
as a subprocess with 8 hostile frames (malformed JSON, valid-JSON-not-JSON-RPC,
unknown method, unknown tool, unknown scenario, missing argument, wrong-typed
argument, top-level array), then a valid `tools/list` probe.

It passes in 0.10 s, which looked far too fast to have spawned a subprocess.
**Measured rather than assumed**: a hand-run three-frame round trip through the
same binary costs **0.024 s** — these paths answer without rendering, so 0.10 s
for ten frames is correct. My suspicion was the apparatus, not the subject
(`no.md` #14).

**Negative control, run in both directions:**

- Binary replaced with a shell stub exiting 1 → **1 failed**,
  `AssertionError: sent 10 frames, got 0 replies`
- Binary restored → **1 passed**, sha256 `6dc2a760…` byte-identical

So the test runs, can fail, and fails for the right reason.
